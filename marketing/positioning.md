# Positioning

Direction from the owner, 2026-11-15. See
[D-003](../work/decisions/D-003-marketing-sells-the-dream.md) and the
[vision](../work/vision.md).

## The feeling we're selling

Two emotions, held together:

1. **"This is the new way of doing things, and I want in."** The shift to
   personal AI is happening. A world where your AI quietly knows what
   matters to you (your kid's school, your club, your favourite café) is
   coming whether or not you join. People should feel the pull of being part
   of it now, in their own city.
2. **"And I can actually do this."** Until now, having an agent that watches
   your world and only bothers you when it matters has been for frontier
   techies: people running self-hosted agent stacks (OpenClaw-type setups),
   wiring up webhooks, MCP servers and home servers. We give the average
   person that same power, with no terminal, no homelab and no cobbling
   together. They should feel clever and ahead, not behind.

The second feeling keeps the first from turning into anxiety. "Get on board"
should feel like an invitation to the front of the line, not fear of being
left behind. Excitement, not FOMO-as-threat.

## The story we tell

**Today:** "Stay informed by the people and places you care about, on your
terms. You decide what's important enough to interrupt you. Everything else
waits quietly until you, or your AI, ask for it."

**Where it's going (the dream):** "Hobart, connected. Every club, school,
café and community group, live and up to date, ready for your AI to ask.
The first city where your personal AI actually knows your town. Built by
the people of Hobart, one channel at a time."

Every early user is a founder of that dream. Make them feel it: founding
publisher badges, "one of the first 100 in Hobart" and similar.

## Who we're talking to

| Audience | What they want | What we lead with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Publishers** (club secretaries, small business owners, community organisers, body corporates) | Fewer "did you see my message?" chases, less admin | Write once and everyone hears it their way. Nobody gets pinged about trivia. Members ask the docs, not you. |
| **Everyday subscribers** | Less noise, never missing what matters | You set the threshold. One quiet inbox for everything local. |
| **AI-curious early adopters** | The frontier setup without the tinkering | Connect your own AI in a minute. Webhooks, MCP and pull mode are all built in. |
| **The tech crowd** (as amplifiers) | Open, well-built, not a walled garden | Open schemas, an API-first design, exportable data, a path to decentralisation. |

The tech crowd isn't the main market, but their approval ("this is actually
a proper MCP setup") gives everyone else confidence.

## Physical presence: the QR code is the campaign

The "scan for live updates" QR poster (idea in `work/ideas.md`) is our
strongest early marketing channel, because every publisher who uses it is
advertising us.

- **It makes the dream visible.** One QR code on a café counter is a
  feature. The same mark on counters, club noticeboards, school gates and
  market stalls all over Hobart is "Hobart, connected", visible in the street
  before anyone reads a word of copy. Seeing it everywhere is what makes
  people feel "this is the new way of doing things".
- **It recruits subscribers for free.** Publishers don't need their
  customers' contact details. Walk-ins become an audience.
- **It recruits publishers.** Other shop owners see the poster next door
  and want one. Each poster carries a small "Get your own" line.
- **A consistent, recognisable mark.** One design and one call to action
  ("Scan for live updates") on every poster, so people learn to look for it,
  like a "tap to pay" sign. The publisher's name and logo sit alongside it;
  ours stays small but constant.
- **The first scan should feel magic.** Scan, pick how much you want to
  hear, done in seconds, with an optional "connect your AI" step. That's the
  moment the average person feels capable rather than out of their depth.
- **Founding-publisher pride.** "A founding Hobart publisher" sticker or
  badge on early posters.

Printing posters and stickers costs money, so the owner decides when and
how much; sessions only design them.

## Tone

- **Confident, warm, local.** Proudly Hobart, not Silicon Valley.
- **Make the complex feel simple.** Never explain MCP to a normal person;
  say "connect your AI". The technical words go on a page for people who
  want them.
- **Show, don't lecture.** Demos of "my AI told me the working bee moved to
  Sunday" beat paragraphs about protocols.
- **Respect attention.** We're the product that interrupts less, so the
  marketing shouldn't shout either.

## Guardrails

- **Today vs tomorrow, always clear.** Sell the dream as where we're going,
  never as what's live. If a feature isn't built, the copy says "coming" or
  doesn't mention it.
- **No fear tactics.** No "you'll be left behind" threats or invented
  scarcity. "Be early" is fine; "act now or else" isn't.
- **No made-up social proof.** Real numbers or none.
- **Privacy is part of the pitch, not fine print.** "Your connections are
  yours. Export or delete any time." That's what lets people trust us with
  their whole local world.
- **No brand imitation.** We can name the category ("self-hosted agent
  setups") but don't borrow anyone's branding or imply endorsement.
